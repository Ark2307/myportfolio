The canonical three pillars of observability — **metrics**, **logs**, and **traces** — were defined in a simpler era. In 2026, with services spanning dozens of clouds and thousands of containers, the original taxonomy is showing cracks.

This isn't a post about dropping the pillars. It's about what goes on top of them.

## The Pillars Are Infrastructure, Not Observability

Metrics tell you *something changed*. Logs tell you *what the system said*. Traces tell you *how a request moved*. But none of them answer the hardest question in production:

> **"Why is this user's experience degraded, and what do I do about it?"**

That question requires correlating all three, plus deployment context, feature flags, infrastructure events, and business signals. Doing that correlation manually during an incident is the industry's biggest unsolved UX problem.

## Structured Events as the Primitive

Charity Majors and the Honeycomb team argued this early: the right primitive is not a metric or a log, but a **wide structured event** — a single JSON blob emitted once per request containing everything interesting about it.

```json
{
  "trace_id": "abc123",
  "user_id": "u_9f3k",
  "service": "api-gateway",
  "endpoint": "/v1/scan",
  "duration_ms": 342,
  "db_queries": 7,
  "cache_hits": 3,
  "plan_tier": "enterprise",
  "region": "us-east-1",
  "deploy_sha": "d3a9f21",
  "feature_flags": ["new_parser_v2"]
}
```

From this single event you can derive metrics (p99 duration by endpoint), logs (filter by user_id), and traces (correlate via trace_id). The event is the source of truth; the pillars are materialized views.

## SLO-Driven Alerting

Alert on *symptoms*, not *causes*. The symptom is user impact — error rate, latency, availability — expressed as an SLO.

```
SLO: 99.9% of /v1/scan requests complete in <500ms over a 28-day window
Error budget: 0.1% × 28 × 24 × 60 = 40.32 minutes
```

Your alerts should fire on **burn rate** — how fast you're consuming your error budget — not raw thresholds. A 1% error rate for 5 minutes burns budget 60× faster than a 0.01% rate held for a week.

Tools like Sloth (for Prometheus), Google's SRE Workbook formulas, and Grafana's SLO plugin make this tractable.

## Continuous Profiling

Traces show you *which service* is slow. Profiling shows you *which function* in that service is slow.

Continuous profilers like **Parca**, **Grafana Pyroscope**, and **Polar Signals** sample call stacks at low overhead (1–2% CPU) and store flame graphs correlated with timestamps. The killer feature: linking a trace span directly to the profile captured during that span.

```
Trace: api-gateway → payments-svc [240ms]
                       ↳ Profile: processPayment() → validateCard() → 180ms
                                   ↳ regex.Match()  ← 170ms (!!!!)
```

That regex match would have taken days to find with logs alone.

## The Correlation Problem

The final frontier is **correlation across signals**. When an alert fires, you want the on-call engineer to land on a dashboard that already knows:

- Which deploy introduced the regression (deployment events)
- Which users are affected (error breakdown by dimension)
- What the flame graph looked like at the time of the incident
- What changed in infrastructure (Kubernetes node restarts, autoscaling events)

OpenTelemetry's `Resource` and `Scope` fields are the connective tissue here — they allow any signal to carry the same set of identifying attributes (service, version, region, deploy SHA).

## What Actually Matters

Observability investment should be proportional to operational risk. The questions to answer before building:

1. Can I tell which deployment caused a regression without a human bisecting git history?
2. Can I find a single user's request path across 12 services in under 2 minutes?
3. Can I explain to a non-engineer why we missed our SLO last month?

If the answer to any of these is "no," you have a gap worth filling. If the answer is "yes" but it costs the team 4 hours of setup per incident, the tooling is the problem.

Build for the 3am incident, not the 2pm demo.
